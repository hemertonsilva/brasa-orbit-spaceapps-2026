Brasa Orbit

A daily calendar of satellite fire detections, MODIS and VIIRS side by side, that shows where the two records agree, where they diverge, and what they cannot tell us — a first step toward harmonizing them: it measures the seasonal gap that any harmonization has to correct.

By Hemerton Silva · Santa Cruz das Palmeiras Local Event (virtual)

Monthly VIIRS / MODIS ratio

How many VIIRS detections for each MODIS detection, month by month. A naive diagnostic, not a harmonization: it mixes pixel size, overpass times and number of satellites.

    Unusual months — each sensor against its own history

    Each cell compares a month’s raw FIRMS detections with the same month in the other two years (2018–2020), separately for MODIS and VIIRS, so a stable sensitivity gap between the sensors drops out. Sensor agreement indicates a consistent relative pattern, not harmonized counts, burned area, or causes. Cells with few detections are not classified. Hover or tap a cell for counts.

        Next step: compare what was actually observed

        Counting detections is not enough to compare sensors: a month can look quiet because of clouds or fewer overpasses, not less fire. The method proposed for Brasa Orbit normalizes detections by the usable area both sensors observed on comparable overpasses:

        Y = 1,000 × ΣD / E

        where D are detections inside the common usable footprint of comparable overpasses and E is the exposure — the observable area accumulated over those overpasses (km² · pass). Months without usable exposure get no rate instead of a false zero. This requires swath-level coverage data and is not implemented in this prototype.

        What these numbers are — and are not

        • Each count is a NASA FIRMS active-fire detection (a pixel flagged with one or more fires or thermal anomalies), filtered to type = 0 (presumed vegetation fire). It is not a count of fires and not burned area.
        • MODIS pixels are about 1 km; VIIRS I-band pixels are 375 m, and the satellites pass at different times. Raw counts from the two sensors are not directly comparable; this view does not harmonize them.
        • Unusual months panel: with only three years, each baseline is two years, so one extreme year pushes the others down; clouds and smoke affect both sensors alike, so their agreement is not independent confirmation of changed fire activity.
        • Dates are the acquisition date in UTC as published by FIRMS. Data: FIRMS standard (archive) yearly country files for Brazil — MODIS version 6.03 and VIIRS S-NPP version 2, as labelled in the files.
        • We acknowledge the use of data and/or imagery from NASA's Fire Information for Resource Management System (FIRMS) (https://earthdata.nasa.gov/firms), part of NASA's Land, Atmosphere Near real-time Capability for Earth observations (LANCE) (https://earthdata.nasa.gov/lance) and NASA's Earth Science Data and Information System (ESDIS).
